# Mecklenburg-Vorpommern 2026: sourced edition

Research cutoff: 14 September 2026. Election: 20 September 2026.

## Status

21 bilingual statements, 125/147 sourced positions. Party coverage: CDU 19/21; SPD 20/21; Bündnis 90 / Die Grünen 18/21; FDP 12/21; AfD 15/21; Die Linke 21/21; BSW 20/21.

This is a completed editorial comparison, not a claim that all positions are known or that the questionnaire is scientifically validated. The selection covers seven requested parties, not every party standing. Remaining unknowns are intentional, not inferred disagreement. Scores exclude unknowns and “don't care”; different denominators limit comparability and must be visible. No database or external answers API.

## Persistent data and sharing

- Current immutable snapshot: dist/mv-data-v2.js (all bilingual text, positions, cell-level source links and source type).
- Detailed sources appear in the results methodology, not next to voting choices.
- New MV results use v=2. Existing v=1 MV links continue to show their original preview; never change dist/mv-data-v1.js.
- Germany and Berlin remain v=1 unchanged.
- Future semantic changes to questions or scores require another version, not edits to either published snapshot.

## Research method

Primary sources: final state programmes linked in the dataset, plus party answers and explanations in the official [Wahl-O-Mat MV 2026](https://www.wahl-o-mat.de/mecklenburgvorpommern2026/). Its published definition files were read without evaluating their JavaScript. Party identifiers were checked against the published party descriptions: 0 SPD, 1 AfD, 2 CDU, 3 Die Linke, 4 Greens, 5 FDP, 12 BSW. App order differs: CDU, SPD, Greens, FDP, AfD, Die Linke, BSW.

Eleven substantively matching questions use published party responses. Other positions are editorial readings of a programme or a party's explanation. Do not automatically invert a party's response to a differently worded question: support for allowing holiday rentals can coexist with targeted municipal restrictions. No long party explanations have been republished.

## Changes from the preview

- Nursery question no longer bundles after-school care where evidence only says Kita.
- Question 2 asks for more staff per child, which is not necessarily the same as a legally smaller group size.
- Questions 3, 4 and 20 now cover special schools, primary-school private phone use and asylum-benefit payment cards. The former rural school locations, universal free lunch and staffing for returns were too weakly evidenced or ambiguous for this compact comparison.
- Pupil transport replaces the specific U27 Deutschlandticket proposal.
- Bus and railway questions omit unconditional low-demand/high-subsidy qualifiers; hospital sites may develop into other healthcare forms.
- Landarztquote is about reserved places, not a numerical increase that continuing policies do not establish.
- Shipyard guarantees replace generic industrial aid; non-profit social housing replaces generic construction subsidy.
- Wind question concerns expansion; local financial participation is not limited to one electricity-tariff mechanism.
- Peatland question concerns support for adapted agriculture; Baltic question concerns nutrient reduction rather than bundling it with fishing restrictions.
- German courses explicitly concern residence-independent access. Camera question concerns expansion in public space, not only narrowly defined crime hotspots.

## Important qualifications

- CDU rail reactivation is a review commitment only: +0.5, not unconditional +1.
- SPD special schools and SPD/AfD public-space surveillance are neutral/mixed in their party responses, scored 0 (half points, not unknown).
- BSW shipyard guarantees exclude military projects; others also attach safeguards. Its published response to the broad guarantees question is +1.
- Opposition to expanded surveillance is not evidence of opposition to every targeted crime-hotspot camera.
- CDU local value creation is insufficiently explicit about direct resident/municipal financial benefit, so question 16 remains null.
- Sources advocating integration only for people likely to remain do not support residence-independent courses.
